What will it feel like? - Challenge 1

Skills gap & constant reskilling pressure

You have more experience than you think.
You just don't know how to sell it yet.
This is a language problem, not an experience problem.
01You can't reframe it.
It rarely maps 1:1, but it transfers. Learn the translation.
02You undersell it.
Most pivoters undersell exactly where they should oversell.
03You let AI hand you a roadmap.
A roadmap is not a skill. Use AI to close the gap, not to describe it.
SNIGDHA · ~3 min · 23:15 The claim is the thesis. Say it, then pause before the traps. Three clicks after that, one per trap. 01 — "Your experience does not map one to one. That is not the same as it not counting. It transfers. It just arrives in the wrong language." 02 — "Every pivoter I have met undersells. You are so worried about being caught overstating that you understate, and then you lose to someone with less experience and better framing." 03 — ★ THE ONE PEOPLE GET WRONG. Use the worked example: "Say you are pivoting into design. You ask AI what a designer needs to know, and it hands you a beautiful list. Design thinking. Core principles. Type, colour, hierarchy. And you screenshot it and you feel productive. That list is a roadmap. A roadmap is not a skill. Knowing what the role demands is not the same as being able to do it. Use AI as a tutor and a collaborator: make it teach you design thinking, critique your work, close the gap. Do not use it to summarise the gap back to you." → then show them exactly what reframing looks like. Next slide.

What will it feel like? - Challenge 2

Job market brutality & hiring dysfunction

The market is broken.
Focus on the controllables.
You cannot fix hiring. You can fix what you take away from every no.
01You don't audit your failures.
Find the leak: resume, interview prep, confidence. Do an RCA and course correct.
02You can't manage your rejections.
Acknowledge the failure.
Use grit and resilience to stay motivated.
03You bet your pivot on a trend.
Some roles might die sooner. Map how the role will evolve with time and changing technologies.

Part Three - The framework

Five courses. In order. You can't skip one.

BBase Knowledge What you already have, named in their language.
RRun Simulation A small, intentional project with one real user.
IImplement Execution Go where it is messy and the decisions are real.
CClaim Ownership Decisions you owned, not titles you held.
KKey Impact What changed because of you. Before, and after.
SNIGDHA · ~4 min · 42:15 ★ THE FRAMEWORK Five clicks. The wall builds course by course, top to bottom, and it spells BRICK as it goes. Roughly 30 seconds per course. Do NOT give each letter a minute, you do not have it, and the worked example on the next slide is what makes it stick. B - the warmest one. This is where their self-doubt lives. Callback: "we did this on the resume slide." R - say the anti-pattern out loud, it is the most useful 15 seconds in Part Three: "random apps with no users, no metrics, no documented decisions. Those do not count. Half of you have a portfolio of exactly that and nobody has told you." I - counterintuitive: seek the MESSY project. Clean projects have no decisions in them, and decisions are the whole point. C - "Nobody is going to give you the title first. You claim the decision, then the title follows." K - handle the objection before they raise it: "what if I have no numbers?" - "took 3 days, now takes 3 hours" IS a metric. ★ THE CLOSING LINE, after all five are up: "You build up. Impact without ownership is a resume lie, and hiring managers can smell it. Each course only holds if the one under it is real."
