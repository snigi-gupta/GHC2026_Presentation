What will it feel like?

Three challenges nobody warns you about.
And how to survive each one.
What will it feel like? - Challenge 1

Skills gap & constant reskilling pressure

You have more experience than you think.
You just don't know how to sell it yet.
This is a language problem, not an experience problem.
01You can't reframe it.
It rarely maps 1:1, but it transfers. Learn the translation.
02You undersell it.
Most pivoters undersell exactly where they should oversell.
03You let AI hand you a roadmap.
A roadmap is not a skill. Use AI to close the gap, not to describe it.

We just showed you what we changed on our resumes. Now: how would Ava take on Challenge 1, reskilling and the constant pressure? First, she figures out her domain and her niche. Domain is her breadth: is she from product, e-commerce, finance, payments, AI? [click x4] Niche is her depth. In product, is it platform, analytics, or ads? In e-commerce, does she work on the seller experience or the buyer experience? In finance, is it P&L? In AI, is it evals, infrastructure, or AI applications? Ava's niche is platform. [click] How do you find yours? Go through your past experiences and look for the pattern across all your projects. In general: you need a domain x niche. Then stay specific to those roles. [click] Ava made this mistake, not us: she applied everywhere, to anything, and it wasted a lot of her time. [click] She made it to the final round for an analytics PM role, and did not get the offer, because someone else had analytics experience and she did not. Even if you pass those interviews, you will not get the offer. All that time and preparation, for an opportunity you were never going to get. Skip it. What actually works: [click] Doubling down on your past experience, your domain, and your niche. That is where you have the most experience. [click] That is where you can say: "I have already done this at a different level, and now I want to bring that experience to the new role." In this day and age, in this market, domain expertise wins over everything else. We cannot emphasize that enough: stick to your domain.
What will it feel like? - Challenge 2

Job market brutality & hiring dysfunction

The market is broken.
Focus on the controllables.
You cannot fix hiring. You can fix what you take away from every no.
01You don't audit your failures.
Find the leak: resume, interview prep, confidence. Do an RCA and course correct.
02You can't manage your rejections.
Acknowledge the failure.
Use grit and resilience to stay motivated.
03You bet your pivot on a trend.
Some roles might die sooner. Map how the role will evolve with time and changing technologies.

So how did Ava get past Challenge 2? At first, every time Ava said she was moving into product, people turned her down. They did not believe she had it in her. [click] Story. She fixed her why. She found her mission, and a very clear way of sharing her journey. That meant fixing her "tell me about yourself," and then saying it everywhere she went. Do not say: "I love users and building products for them, so I became a PM." That is a cliche. Say something like: "I realized that with the domain expertise I have built over X years, I can help build products that actually solve users' pain points in this space." That told people she was dedicated to the pivot, and had extreme clarity on why she was doing it. Same on LinkedIn: do not write "Aspiring Product Manager." Write your superpower instead. For example: "Leveraging analytics to build products users trust." [click] Skill. Ava realized she was missing experience that showed her product skills. So she built a product from scratch, and talked about it. She started spotting improvements across different products and wrote blogs about them. It began as a personal project, then she saw she could grow it. She figured out what most people wanted to know and understand, which meant she was really learning to understand her users and her audience. [click] Practice. Ava faced her failures. Every time she did not make it through an interview, she did more mocks, with strangers and with her product management community. They worked on product empathy, product sense, user understanding, and customer segmentation. That gave her a framework for how to think in interviews, which is essential no matter what role you are pivoting from or into.

Here is what that staircase looked like for Ava. [click] Build failed. Ava built an AI "mind palace," a memory tool running on AI automation. She was confident she could get the token usage, the AI cost, down far enough to make it work. She couldn't, and that one assumption killed the build. The lesson: test your riskiest assumption first. That is a product lesson, and she learned it by failing. [click] Build good. A UX layer on top of a developer tool she already used: an existing GitHub skill. Solid, useful, solved a real problem for her. But it did not change how anyone saw her. Useful is not the same as memorable. [click] Build great. AI agents that report their own traffic and evidence. Basically: how do you actually verify what a coding agent is sending and doing? That one gave people a new way to think about a space they thought they already understood. Great builds change how others think. Failed, good, or great: every one of them taught her something.

How to build credibility for your pivot role?

The BRICK framework.
How to build credibility for your pivot role? - The framework

Earn the role you’ve never held, brick by brick.

B
Base Knowledge
Leverage what you already know. Your past experience is not “irrelavant”; it’s transferable.
R
Run Simulation
Create small, intentional projects. Demonstration beats theoretical knowledge.
I
Implement Execution
Seek messy, high-leverage opportunities where decisions matter. Credibility grows where outcomes are real, not hypothetical.
C
Claim Ownership
Highlight moments where you took responsibility over decisions, not titles.
K
Key Impact
Translate your work into measurable results. Impact is the proof that your stack works and makes your credibility undeniable.
